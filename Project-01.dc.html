DropBy teardown

A UX and branding teardown of a live location-based social app, redesigning its onboarding and identity to fix trust and retention gaps.

Role Product lead
Timeline Jul — Aug 2025
Team A peer team at a designathon
Outcome Pitched to the DropBy team

The problem

DropBy is a location-based social app that lets people “drop” their location and meet others nearby. It launched with weak branding, a cluttered UI and nothing to bring people back. Our brief was to work out why an app solving a real problem wasn’t becoming a habit, and fix it.

Research

We surveyed 61 students and young professionals and built two personas from it: Akash, a remote worker new to Bangalore, and Harini, a final-year student. Two findings shaped the redesign.

68%said knowing someone’s mood or intent would make approaching a stranger feel safer.
51%had wanted to talk to someone nearby but didn’t. The barrier was activation, not desire.

We also audited the live app (cluttered top bar, low contrast, an oversized static map) and mapped DropBy against social, dating and networking apps.

Decisions

Gamify onboarding instead of just streamlining it

Rather than shortening sign-up, we added progress cues and let people build a real profile (hobbies, vibe). The research showed the friction was emotional, a fear of misreading a stranger, not procedural.

“Playful yet curious” over “adventure and bold”

We chose calmer colours and softer illustration. A high-energy UI would feel too intense for daily, low-stakes use and would undercut the safety concerns users raised.

What shipped

A refreshed identity (colour palette, wordmark, typography) and a redesigned onboarding and core flow: sign-up, profile setup, home, map, events and an AI chatbot concept. Alongside it, a prioritised feature set:

Verified badgeDrop streaksEvent notificationsGender and safety filters

Impact

A full identity system, a redesigned onboarding flow and a prioritised feature roadmap, grounded in 61 survey responses and a direct UI audit. We presented the work to the DropBy team.

What I'd redo

We designed for retention before we’d proven activation. The survey showed people hesitate to even start, which is an activation problem, yet several of our ideas (streaks, DropBy points) are retention mechanics. I’d nail the first-conversation moment before building loyalty loops on top of it.

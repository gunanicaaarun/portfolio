Final year project — 2024

Real-time ISL recognition

A hybrid CNN-RNN model for recognising Indian Sign Language in emergency situations, benchmarked against three other architectures to find the most viable real-time approach.

RoleModel design, implementation and evaluation (co-author)
Timeline2023 — 2024
TeamWith Pavithra Manikandan, guided by Dr. J. K. Josephine Julina
Outcome97% accuracy. Paper submitted, not yet published

The problem

ISL has far less tooling and data than ASL, and existing research stopped at static images of alphabets and numerals. Nothing addressed real-time, sequential signs where communication is time-critical. We scoped it narrowly: could a model reliably recognise, in real time, the words that matter most when someone can’t speak — alive, dead, sick, fast, loud, mother, healthy?

Prior work and data

We reviewed nine prior ISL recognition papers. None tested in real time, none modelled signs as motion over time, and accuracy on alphabet-only tasks topped out between 70 and 85%.

4,287videos in INCLUDE, the first public ISL dataset, recorded with the deaf community
263signs in the full dataset
7emergency-relevant words we trained on

Approach

Video over images

Most prior work classified static images. We worked from video to capture how a sign moves, not just the hand’s shape at one instant, which is closer to how sign language is actually used.

Benchmark four architectures, not one

We compared CNN alone, CNN+RNN, CNN+RNN with MediaPipe, and YOLOv8, and let accuracy and loss decide. YOLOv8 located gestures precisely but couldn’t hold precision across confidence thresholds; the simpler MediaPipe combination generalised better.

7 words, not 263

Given time and compute, we chose depth and reliability on a use-case-relevant subset over breadth.

The final model

A hybrid CNN-RNN using MediaPipe hand tracking, recognising 7 emergency-relevant ISL signs in real time. It was the best of the four architectures tested.

Results

CNN
63%
CNN + RNN
80%
CNN + RNN + MediaPipe
97%

The final model reached 97% accuracy and 0.1 loss, with F1 scores above 0.87 on every class. The findings are co-authored into a paper that has been submitted and is not yet published.

What I'd redo

We never tested the model’s real-time output with ISL signers. The dataset was recorded with community input, but for a project motivated by accessibility, not validating with the people it’s meant to serve is the biggest gap.

Seven signs is a proof of concept, not a deployable tool. Real use in an emergency needs far more vocabulary, and ISL varies by region; we trained on one dataset’s signing style and never checked against that variation.
